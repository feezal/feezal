# B113 — Gap snapping: draw full-canvas helper lines so the gap arrows meet them at 90° ✅ fixed
*Archived roadmap item — Bugs. Open items: [../ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md) · Index: [README.md](README.md)*

**Reported (08/2026).** The [U89](U89.md) gap arrows floated in the gaps alone. Wanted: like the edge-snap guides, gap snapping should also draw **helper lines across the whole canvas** — the edges the gap is measured against — so each dotted arrow visibly ends ON a line at a right angle, the Figma-style reading "this span runs between these two edges".

## What gets a line

Every candidate carries two spans — the gap a neighbour pair already has (`measured`) and the one being proposed (`proposed`) — each with a `from` and a `to` on the measured axis. Those four coordinates are exactly the edges the proposal is derived from, so each one gets a line, **perpendicular** to the arrow: an x-axis span (a horizontal arrow) ends on full-height verticals, a y-axis span on full-width horizontals.

Ends are deduped by rounded coordinate. A rhythm candidate whose proposed span starts where the measured one ends would otherwise draw the same line twice, and two dotted lines on the same pixel read heavier than one.

Drawn in the guides' dotted **grey**, not the arrows' accent colour: the lines are context for the proposal, not the proposal itself, and the accent has to stay on the thing that is actually being offered.

Shipped with [B112](B112.md) as one pass, so the lines appear and disappear with the arrows on both axes at once — up to four verticals and four horizontals, from two `GAP_LINE_COUNT` pools.

**Verified** in the real editor ([editor-snap-guides.test.js](../../www/test-e2e/editor-snap-guides.test.js)): a drag where both rhythms apply shows four verticals and four horizontals, each measured to span the canvas on its axis; a drag with no proposal shows none. The dedupe and the per-axis coordinate translation are pinned in [feezal-sidebar-inspector-logic.test.js](../../www/test/feezal-sidebar-inspector-logic.test.js), where the geometry can be checked to the pixel without a real drag.

**Superseded in part by [B116](B116.md) ✅** (08/2026, same month): drawing all four ends turned out to be three lines too many — the arrows already carry the between-which-edges story. Only the line at the dragged element's own edge survives, one per axis. Everything else here still holds: full-canvas, perpendicular, dotted grey, appearing and disappearing with the arrows.

**Relates:** [U89](U89.md) ✅, N11 (the guide-line renderer this matches), [B112](B112.md) ✅ (both axes — implemented together), [B116](B116.md) ✅ (prunes the four lines to one).
