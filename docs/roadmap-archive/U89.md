# U89 — Equal-gap smart guides during drag ✅ done
*Archived roadmap item — Editor UX. Open items: [../ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md) · Index: [README.md](README.md)*

Carried out of **U83** ✅ (align & distribute), which covers the explicit operation but not the live hint. Dragging an element now proposes the gap its neighbours already use, so a row can be laid out by hand without invoking Distribute at all.

## Neighbour-gap propagation

When the dragged element approaches a stationary one, the detector looks at THAT element's gap to its own next neighbour on the other side and proposes the same gap on this side. Two cards 16 px apart, drag a third towards them, and it lands 16 px out — rows grow with a consistent rhythm without anyone measuring anything.

`www/src/feezal-canvas-gaps.js` is pure geometry: boxes in, candidates out. It answers the item's two open questions:

1. **How far to look — the adjacent pair only.** A candidate needs an anchor and that anchor's other-side neighbour. Scanning the whole row would offer gaps that are not visually adjacent to the pointer, which reads as the canvas guessing rather than helping.
2. **Same row/column only.** A gap between boxes that do not overlap on the cross axis is not a gap anyone can see, so the lane-overlap test is what keeps proposals meaningful.

Ties break toward the **smaller** gap: with two rhythms equally close, the tighter one is the likelier continuation, and deciding deterministically stops the guide flickering between two answers on consecutive frames.

## Gap arrows

Two dotted spans with end ticks and the px value: one over the gap the neighbour pair already has, one over the gap being proposed under the drag. Both are drawn at the **dragged element's own centre line** rather than at the neighbours', so the eye compares two spans at the same height — which is the point of showing them. They use the selection colour rather than the guides' grey, because this is an active proposal, not a passive alignment hint (and are therefore excluded from the grid-colour sync).

## Snapping

Equal-gap and edge snapping compete on **distance**. The first implementation let any in-range edge win outright, which was wrong and the live test caught it: with `range = 24`, an edge 21 px away beat a gap proposal 5 px away, so the card landed flush against its neighbour instead of on the rhythm. Edges now win only an exact tie — aligning is a stronger intent than continuing a rhythm, but proximity decides first.

## Verification

`test/feezal-canvas-gaps.test.js` (12) pins the geometry: propagation on both sides and both axes, both spans reported and equal, other-lane elements ignored, a lone neighbour proposing nothing, touching/overlapping pairs ignored, the adjacent pair used rather than the whole row, nearest-within-range selection, the tie-break, and stability.

Live-driven in a real editor: three cards at 40 / 156 / 520 with a 16 px gap; dragging the third towards the pair lands it at exactly **272** (= 256 + 16) with both arrows reading `16`, and the arrows disappear on drop.
